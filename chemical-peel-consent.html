About chemical peels

The peels we offer behave very differently from one another. This matters for what you should expect.

How peels work

A chemical solution is applied to the skin to break the bonds between surface cells and stimulate repair beneath. Depending on the formulation and depth, this can improve texture, tone, pigmentation, congestion, fine lines and scarring, and prompt new collagen production.

Not all of our peels make you peel — and the ones that do, do it visibly. This is the single thing patients most often get wrong. Please read the descriptions below and be clear which you are having, because the downtime is completely different.

The peels we offer

BioRePeel

An Italian biphasic peel combining TCA with salicylic, lactobionic and other acids, plus amino acids and vitamins. The oil phase protects the surface of the skin while the acids work underneath, which is why it is well tolerated and why most people do not visibly peel. Available for the face, neck and décolletage, and in a body formulation for areas such as the back, arms and legs. Typically a course of 4 to 6 sessions, 1 to 2 weeks apart.

PRX-T33

A TCA-based treatment combined with hydrogen peroxide and kojic acid, massaged into the skin rather than left to work on the surface. The hydrogen peroxide protects the epidermis, so it stimulates the deeper layers without causing surface injury. There is essentially no peeling and no light sensitivity afterwards, which makes it one of the few treatments that can be done at any time of year and immediately before an event. Typically 4 to 6 sessions, a week apart.

Jessner's peel

A medium-depth peel combining salicylic acid, lactic acid and resorcinol. This one genuinely peels. The skin frosts during treatment, then over the following days darkens, tightens and flakes away — usually starting around day two or three and lasting up to a week. It is the most effective of our peels for pigmentation, sun damage and acne scarring, but it requires real downtime and cannot be done casually before an event.

Obagi Blue Peel Radiance

A superficial salicylic acid peel with a blue base, giving brightness and smoothness with light flaking over three to five days. A good introduction to peeling, and effective for congestion, dullness and uneven tone. Often used as a course.

What to expect generally

  • Tingling, stinging or heat during application. This is normal and settles quickly.
  • Redness afterwards, ranging from mild to like moderate sunburn depending on the peel.
  • Tightness and dryness over the following days.
  • Results build with a course. A single peel gives a freshening; a course changes the skin.
  • Sun protection afterwards is not optional. Skipping it is the most common cause of pigmentation problems.

Risks and side effects

These vary considerably by peel. The deeper the peel, the greater the effect and the greater the risk.

Common — expect these

  • Stinging, tingling or heat during application
  • Redness afterwards, from mild to like moderate sunburn
  • Tightness and dryness for several days
  • Flaking or peeling, from barely noticeable to obvious depending on the peel
  • Temporary sensitivity of the treated skin
  • Skin looking worse before it looks better, particularly with deeper peels

Less common

  • Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation — a darker patch after healing. This is the most common significant complication and the risk is higher in deeper skin tones and where sun protection is not used properly.
  • Temporary lightening of the skin
  • A breakout or purge in the first week or two
  • Cold sore outbreak, where you are prone to them
  • Prolonged redness
  • Swelling, particularly around the eyes
  • Milia — small white bumps
  • Flare of an underlying condition such as eczema, rosacea or melasma
  • Uneven peeling or patchy results
  • Infection, occasionally needing antibiotics

Rare

  • Scarring, including raised or keloid scarring in those prone to it
  • Permanent pigment change, lighter or darker
  • Chemical burn from an unexpectedly strong reaction
  • Allergic reaction to a component of the peel
  • Systemic effects from absorption of resorcinol or salicylic acid where large areas are treated
Sun protection is the difference between a good result and a pigmentation problem. Freshly peeled skin has lost some of its natural protection and its pigment cells are primed to react. Daily SPF 50, no sunbeds, and genuine sun avoidance for at least two weeks is not advice — it is a condition of having the treatment. The most common reason a peel goes wrong is someone going out in the sun afterwards.
Contact the clinic promptly on 0800 772 0137 if you develop: blistering; skin that weeps, crusts or breaks down; spreading redness, heat or discharge; a temperature and feeling generally unwell; severe pain rather than tightness; or redness worsening rather than settling after 72 hours.

Alternatives

You are entitled to know what else could achieve your goal, including doing nothing.

  • No treatment. Always a reasonable choice. There is no harm in declining.
  • Medical skincare and daily sun protection. For pigmentation especially, what you do at home matters more than what we do in clinic. A peel without a home routine is a short-lived result.
  • A gentler peel than the one being considered, or a course building up in strength.
  • Medical microneedling, which stimulates from within rather than resurfacing from the surface, and is safer in deeper skin tones.
  • Laser or IPL, for certain pigmentation and vascular concerns — though with a higher risk of pigmentation change in darker skin.
  • Prescription topical treatment, particularly for melasma and acne, where a peel alone will not hold the result.
  • Injectable regenerative treatments, where the concern is skin quality and firmness rather than surface texture and tone.
If you have melasma, please read this. Melasma is driven by heat, light and inflammation, and peels can make it worse as easily as better. It needs a careful, layered plan involving prescription topicals, rigorous sun protection and conservative treatment. We will treat it, but we will not treat it aggressively, and we will be honest that it is managed rather than cured.

Aftercare

What you do in the following week decides most of your result.

Do not pick, peel, scrub or pull at flaking skin. Let it shed on its own, even when it is hanging off and driving you mad. Pulling skin away before it is ready is the single most common cause of scarring and pigmentation after a peel.

For the first 24 hours

  • Leave the skin alone. No makeup unless we have said otherwise.
  • No exercise, saunas, steam rooms, swimming or very hot showers.
  • No touching your face with unwashed hands.
  • Cleanse gently with lukewarm water and only the products we advise.
  • For BioRePeel, follow the specific instruction we give you about when to wash the product off.

Until the skin has fully recovered

  • SPF 50 every single day, reapplied, and a hat where you can.
  • No sunbeds, no deliberate sun exposure, no self-tan.
  • No retinoids, acids, exfoliants or scrubs.
  • No waxing, threading, hair removal cream or electrolysis in the area.
  • No other facial treatments, laser or microneedling.
  • Moisturise generously and often. Dry, tight skin peels worse and heals worse.
  • Drink plenty of water.

Contact us if

  • You develop blistering, weeping or crusting
  • The skin becomes hot, increasingly painful or produces discharge
  • Redness spreads or worsens after 72 hours
  • You feel generally unwell with a temperature
  • A cold sore develops
  • Anything at all concerns you
