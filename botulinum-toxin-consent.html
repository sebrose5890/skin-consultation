About the treatment

What botulinum toxin is, and what it realistically does.

How it works

Botulinum toxin type A is a purified protein. Injected in very small, precise amounts into specific muscles, it temporarily blocks the nerve signal telling that muscle to contract. The muscle relaxes, and the lines created by repeatedly contracting it soften.

Timings

  • You will usually notice a change from around 3 to 5 days.
  • The full effect is reached at 21 days. The result should not be judged before then.
  • It typically lasts 3 to 4 months, though this varies considerably between individuals and by area treated.
  • The effect wears off gradually. Muscles return to how they were beforehand — they do not become weaker over time or worse than before treatment.

What it will not do

  • It softens lines caused by muscle movement. It will not remove static lines — those present when your face is completely still.
  • It does not replace lost volume, lift loose skin, improve skin quality or treat pigmentation.
  • Deep, long-established lines may improve but are unlikely to disappear, and may need several rounds of treatment before you see the full benefit.
  • Complete symmetry is not achievable. Most faces are naturally asymmetric, and treatment does not correct this.
A review at 21 days is part of the treatment, not a sign something went wrong. Everyone metabolises this differently and small adjustments are normal. Please contact us if you would like to be seen.

Risks and side effects

Every treatment carries risk. These are set out in full so you can weigh them properly.

Common — expect these

  • Redness, small raised bumps and mild swelling at injection points, settling within an hour or two
  • Bruising, which can last up to two weeks and is more likely around the eyes
  • Tenderness at the injection sites
  • Headache in the first 24 to 48 hours
  • A feeling of heaviness or tightness in the treated area as it takes effect

Less common

  • Asymmetry — one side responding differently to the other
  • An unwanted brow position, including a raised or arched outer brow
  • Drooping of the eyelid (ptosis), usually temporary and resolving as the effect wears off. Drops can be prescribed to help.
  • Drooping or uneven smile, where the lower face is treated
  • Dry or watering eyes
  • Flu-like symptoms in the days afterwards
  • Localised muscle weakness beyond the intended area
  • The result being weaker or stronger than you hoped, or wearing off sooner than expected
  • No response at all, including because of antibodies formed against the product
  • Infection at an injection site

Rare, and specific to certain areas

  • Double or blurred vision
  • Difficulty swallowing, or neck weakness — associated with treatment of the neck and lower face
  • Difficulty chewing or a temporary change in bite, following jawline treatment
  • Speech changes, following treatment around the mouth
  • Increased sweating in a different area, following treatment for excessive sweating
  • Allergic reaction, which very rarely can be severe
Spread of effect beyond the injection site Very rarely, the effect of botulinum toxin has spread beyond the area treated, causing symptoms including muscle weakness, difficulty swallowing, difficulty speaking or difficulty breathing. This can occur hours to weeks afterwards. Reported cases have overwhelmingly involved high doses used for medical conditions rather than the small doses used in aesthetic treatment, and it is extremely rare in cosmetic use — but it is a recognised risk and you must be told about it. If you develop any of these symptoms, seek urgent medical attention and contact the clinic.

Alternatives

You are entitled to know what else could achieve your goal, including doing nothing.

  • No treatment. Always a reasonable choice. Lines will continue to develop gradually with age and muscle movement, but there is no harm in declining.
  • Medical skincare. Retinoids, vitamin C and consistent sun protection improve skin quality and fine surface lines, though not lines caused by muscle movement.
  • Resurfacing treatments such as peels or microneedling, which target texture, tone and static lines rather than movement.
  • Skin boosters and injectable hydration, which improve skin quality rather than reducing muscle activity.
  • Dermal filler, where the concern is volume loss or a static line rather than movement. This is a different treatment with a different risk profile.
  • Surgical options, where the concern is significant skin laxity that no injectable treatment will address.

Aftercare

Following this properly reduces the risk of the product moving where it shouldn't.

For the first 4 hours

  • Stay upright. Do not lie flat or bend forward for long periods.
  • Do not rub, massage or press the treated area.
  • Gentle facial movement — frowning, raising your brows — is fine and may help.

For the first 24 hours

  • No strenuous exercise, saunas, steam rooms or very hot baths.
  • No alcohol, which increases bruising.
  • Avoid makeup over the injection points where possible.

For two weeks

  • No facials, massage, microneedling, peels or laser over the treated area.
  • Avoid lying face down on a massage or physiotherapy couch — pressure through a face hole is a recognised cause of eyelid and brow complications.
  • Avoid sunbeds and prolonged heat exposure.

Contact us if

  • You develop drooping of an eyelid or brow
  • You develop signs of infection — spreading redness, heat, pain or discharge
  • You are unhappy with the result at 21 days
  • Anything concerns you at all

Call 0800 772 0137. For difficulty swallowing, speaking or breathing, seek urgent medical attention first.
