What to expect

Please read this properly before consenting.

How treatment works

  • Treatment is a weekly injection you give yourself, using a pre-filled pen. We will show you how.
  • You start on a low dose and increase gradually over several months. This is deliberate — it reduces side effects.
  • Medication is licensed for use alongside a reduced-calorie diet and increased activity, not instead of them.
  • You will be reviewed regularly. Continuing is a clinical decision each time, not an automatic repeat.

Side effects

  • Common: nausea, constipation, diarrhoea, vomiting, reflux, burping, fatigue, headache, dizziness. Most settle as your body adjusts and are worst after a dose increase.
  • Less common: gallstones, hair thinning, changes in taste, injection site reactions, low mood.
  • Serious but uncommon: pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, kidney problems from dehydration, severe allergic reaction, and low blood sugar if you take insulin or certain diabetes medications.
Stop and seek urgent medical help if you develop: severe, persistent abdominal pain, particularly if it spreads to your back and comes with vomiting — this can be pancreatitis. Also seek help for signs of a severe allergic reaction, or if you cannot keep fluids down.

Things people are not usually told

  • You will lose muscle as well as fat. Protein intake and resistance training matter enormously here. Losing weight without them costs you strength and metabolic rate.
  • Your face will change. Fat is lost from the cheeks and temples, and skin loses the support beneath it. It is well recognised and it catches people out.
  • Weight regain after stopping is common, particularly where eating and activity habits have not changed alongside.
  • It does not work for everyone. Some people respond very well, some modestly, some not at all.
  • Alcohol tolerance often changes, and many people find they lose interest in it.
