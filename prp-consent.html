About the treatment

What PRP is and what happens on the day.

What happens

  • We take a small sample of your blood from your arm, in the same way as a routine blood test.
  • It is spun in a centrifuge using the Cellenis® system, which uses a gel separator to isolate the platelet rich plasma cleanly from the red cells.
  • The resulting plasma is concentrated in platelets, which carry the growth factors your body uses to repair tissue.
  • It is then either injected into the treatment area, applied during microneedling, or both — depending on what we are treating.

How it works

Platelets release growth factors that trigger your body's own repair processes — stimulating fibroblasts to make collagen and elastin, improving microcirculation, and supporting tissue regeneration. Because it is made entirely from your own blood, there is no risk of allergic reaction or rejection.

PRP is not a filler. It adds no meaningful volume and will not fill a hollow, a line or a groove, or lift descended tissue. What it changes is the quality of the skin or the health of the hair follicle. If volume or structure is what concerns you, a different treatment is appropriate and we will say so.

Course and timings

  • A course of 3 sessions, 4 to 6 weeks apart for skin. Hair treatment is usually 3 to 4 monthly sessions to begin with.
  • Each session is charged separately.
  • Results build gradually from around 4 to 6 weeks and continue developing for several months.
  • Maintenance is generally recommended every 6 to 12 months for skin, and every 3 to 6 months for hair.
  • A single session will not give the result described here. The course is the treatment.

Results depend partly on you

Because the active ingredient is your own blood, the quality of your result depends in part on your age, general health, smoking status, diet and medication. This means outcomes are less predictable between individuals than with a manufactured product, and we cannot promise a specific result.

Risks and side effects

Because the product is your own blood, allergic reaction and rejection are not a concern. The risks are those of taking the blood and of the injections themselves.

From taking the blood

  • Bruising, tenderness or a small lump at the needle site in your arm
  • Feeling faint, light-headed or nauseous — tell us immediately if you do
  • Occasionally more than one attempt is needed to obtain a sample

Common after treatment — expect these

  • Redness, warmth and mild swelling for 24 to 72 hours
  • Bruising, which can be significant around the eyes and last up to two weeks
  • Tenderness at the treated area
  • Small raised bumps at injection points, settling within hours
  • On the scalp, tenderness and mild tightness for a day or two

Less common

  • Swelling lasting longer than a couple of days, particularly under the eyes
  • Small lumps or unevenness persisting for a week or two
  • Temporary pigment change at an injection point
  • Cold sore outbreak, where you are prone to them
  • Infection at an injection site, occasionally needing antibiotics
  • Headache or feeling washed out for a day afterwards
  • A temporary increase in hair shedding, where the scalp is treated
  • Little or no visible improvement

Rare

  • A persistent nodule requiring treatment
  • Abscess or deeper infection
  • Nerve irritation causing altered sensation, usually temporary
  • Blockage of a blood vessel by injected material
  • Scarring

Alternatives

You are entitled to know what else could achieve your goal, including doing nothing.

  • No treatment. Always a reasonable choice. There is no harm in declining.
  • Medical skincare and daily sun protection, which underpin every other treatment.
  • Polynucleotides, which regenerate by a different mechanism, do not require a blood draw, and have a larger published evidence base.
  • Sunekos or Profhilo, for skin quality using hyaluronic acid and amino acids rather than your own blood.
  • Medical microneedling on its own, or with a different infused active.
  • Dermal filler, where the real concern is a hollow or lost volume. PRP will not do this.
  • For hair loss: prescribed medication such as minoxidil or finasteride where appropriate, polynucleotide scalp treatments, nutritional assessment, or referral for a dermatological opinion. PRP works best alongside these rather than instead of them.
  • Surgical opinion, including hair transplant, where thinning is advanced.

Aftercare

What you do afterwards affects how well the growth factors work.

Straight after

  • Keep the plaster on your arm for a couple of hours and avoid heavy lifting with that arm for the rest of the day.
  • Eat and drink normally. Tell us before you leave if you feel at all light-headed.

For the first 24 hours

  • No makeup over the treated area.
  • No strenuous exercise, saunas, steam rooms or very hot baths.
  • No alcohol, which increases bruising.
  • Do not rub or massage the area.
  • If your scalp was treated, do not wash your hair, and avoid hats and helmets.
  • Sleep on your back with your head slightly raised if you can.
Avoid anti-inflammatory medication. Ibuprofen, naproxen and aspirin blunt the platelet activity this treatment depends on. Please avoid them for 48 hours before and 48 hours after each session where you safely can. Paracetamol is fine. If you take aspirin or an anti-inflammatory on prescription, do not stop it — tell us instead.

For the first week

  • Avoid retinoids and acids over the treated area for a few days.
  • Cool compresses help with swelling and bruising. Never put ice directly on the skin.
  • Arnica can help with bruising.
  • Drink plenty of water and eat well — you are asking your body to repair tissue.

For two weeks

  • No facials, massage, microneedling, peels or laser over the area.
  • Avoid dental treatment where possible, including the hygienist.
  • Avoid vaccinations where the timing is within your control.
  • Daily SPF, and avoid sunbeds and deliberate sun exposure.
