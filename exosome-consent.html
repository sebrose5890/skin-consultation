What exosomes are

And, importantly, how they are and are not used here.

What they are

Exosomes are microscopic vesicles — tiny packages released naturally by cells. They carry growth factors, proteins, lipids and genetic signalling material, and act as messengers that tell surrounding cells how to repair and regenerate.

In aesthetics they are used to accelerate healing after microneedling and to enhance the skin's repair response, with the aim of improving texture, hydration, radiance and overall skin quality.

We apply exosomes topically only. They are never injected. In the UK, injecting exosomes — intradermally, subcutaneously or intravenously — is not lawful. The MHRA treats injected exosomes as a medicinal product, and no exosome-based injectable holds a UK marketing authorisation.

At Sebastian Rose, exosomes are applied to the surface of the skin during and immediately after your microneedling treatment. The micro-channels created by the needling allow the product to be absorbed far more effectively than it would be from a serum applied to intact skin. Nothing is injected.

If any clinic offers to inject exosomes, or describes them as a skin booster given by needle, that is a clear warning sign and you should decline.

The product we use

We use only CE-marked exosome products sourced through verified UK suppliers. We do not use human-derived or donor tissue-derived exosome products. Your practitioner will tell you exactly which product is being used and can show you the packaging if you would like to see it.

The evidence position

Exosomes are a genuinely promising area and there is a growing body of research behind them, including split-face studies showing improvements in skin texture, hydration, elasticity and pigmentation when combined with microneedling or laser. However, the evidence base in aesthetics is still emerging rather than established, and no exosome product is approved as a medicine by the MHRA or the FDA anywhere in the world.

We think they are a worthwhile addition for the right patient, which is why we offer them — but we would rather you knew the evidence position than assumed it was equivalent to a licensed treatment.

What to expect

  • Results are typically subtle and gradual rather than dramatic.
  • Many people notice faster settling of redness and a better recovery from the microneedling itself.
  • Improvements in skin quality build over the weeks following treatment, alongside the collagen response from the needling.
  • As with microneedling generally, a course produces considerably more than a single session.

Risks and alternatives

Specific to the exosome component. The risks of microneedling itself are set out on your microneedling consent.

Common

  • Nothing beyond what you would expect from the microneedling itself — redness, mild swelling, tightness and flaking as the skin recovers

Less common

  • Irritation, stinging or a reaction to the product itself
  • Redness lasting longer than it would from microneedling alone
  • Small spots or a temporary breakout
  • No noticeable additional benefit over microneedling on its own

Rare

  • Allergic or hypersensitivity reaction to a component of the product
  • Prolonged inflammatory reaction
  • Contact dermatitis
Because the micro-channels are open, anything applied penetrates more deeply than it would normally. That is exactly why exosomes work well this way — and also why the product used must be appropriate for the purpose, sterile, and from a verified source. It is the same reason we ask you not to put your own skincare on afterwards.

Alternatives

  • Microneedling on its own, without any add-on. Entirely reasonable, and effective in its own right.
  • PRP, using your own blood, infused during the same treatment.
  • Polynucleotides, which have a larger published evidence base and can be injected lawfully.
  • Other infused actives such as NCTF, Sunekos or WOW Fusion serums.
  • No treatment at all. Always a reasonable choice.
