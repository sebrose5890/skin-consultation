What we are dissolving

The single most important thing we need to establish is what is actually in there.

Hyaluronidase only dissolves hyaluronic acid. It does nothing to anything else. It will not touch Sculptra, Radiesse, Ellanse, Juvelook, Lenisna, silicone, PMMA, Aquamid, Bio-Alcamid, threads, fat transfer, or any permanent or semi-permanent product. If what you have is not hyaluronic acid, dissolving will not work and we will have injected you for nothing.

If you do not know what you had, say so. That is a useful and honest answer, and it changes how we approach this.
Please include everything you have ever had injected in this area, however long ago, even if you think it has gone. Bring any records, receipts, product names or batch stickers to your appointment.

What dissolving can and cannot do

Please read this section particularly carefully. Most disappointment after dissolving comes from expectations set here.

1. We cannot dissolve "just a little bit". Hyaluronidase spreads through tissue. It cannot be aimed at a specific millimetre of product and stopped there. Once it is in, it works on whatever hyaluronic acid it reaches. You should assume that everything in the treated area will be affected, not just the part you dislike.
2. It will not spot reduce. We cannot take a fraction off one side, shave a lump down, or make something slightly smaller while leaving the rest. If you want a subtle reduction, dissolving is not the tool for that — and anyone who tells you otherwise is not being straight with you. The realistic choice is usually to dissolve the area properly and start again if you want to.
3. You will very likely look worse before you look better. Immediately after dissolving you can expect swelling, and once that settles the area will look flatter, emptier and often older than it did before. This is the point of the treatment, but it is still a shock. Many people find the first two weeks genuinely upsetting.

Filler may also have been masking volume loss you had not noticed. Removing it can reveal ageing that was always there. That is not a complication — it is your face without the filler.
4. Your own hyaluronic acid is affected, but only temporarily. Hyaluronidase does break down the hyaluronic acid your body makes naturally, as well as the filler. This is why the area can look particularly deflated at first. It is not permanent. Your body replaces its own hyaluronic acid within a day or two, and it does not damage your tissue, your collagen or your skin's ability to make hyaluronic acid in future. Nothing is permanently removed.

Other things to expect

  • More than one session is common. Dense, long-standing or heavily layered filler often needs two or three treatments, usually 2 to 4 weeks apart. Each is charged separately.
  • The result is not fully predictable. We cannot promise how much will dissolve, how evenly, or exactly how you will look afterwards.
  • Some product may remain, particularly if it is old, deeply placed or of a type that resists dissolving.
  • Skin may not fully retract. Where a large volume has stretched tissue over a long time — lips especially — the skin may stay looser than it was before you ever had filler.
  • The final appearance takes 2 to 4 weeks to settle. Do not judge it in the first fortnight.
  • If you want filler again, we would normally wait at least 2 weeks, and often longer, before re-treating.

Your appointment

This takes considerably longer than a filler appointment. Please plan for it.

Allow around two hours in clinic, and do not book anything immediately afterwards.

What will happen, in order

  • 1. Patch test. A small amount of hyaluronidase is injected into your forearm.
  • 2. You wait 25 to 30 minutes in clinic. We watch for any sign of a reaction — redness, a raised weal, itching, or anything systemic.
  • 3. If the patch test is clear, we proceed and dissolve the treated area.
  • 4. You then wait a further 30 minutes in clinic. This is not optional. It is because a reaction can occur after the main dose even when the patch test was clear, and we want you here rather than in a car park if it does.
  • 5. We review you before you leave.
A clear patch test reduces the risk. It does not eliminate it. A negative patch test is reassuring, and it is why we do it — but reactions have occurred after a clear test, including in people who have had hyaluronidase uneventfully before. Sensitisation can develop with repeated exposure, so a previous uneventful treatment does not guarantee the next one.

We keep adrenaline and full emergency equipment on site and our practitioners are trained in managing anaphylaxis.
We would prefer you did not drive yourself immediately afterwards, and would rather you were not alone for the first few hours.
Please do not book this within a month of anything important. Swelling, bruising and the flat appearance afterwards all take time to settle.

Safety screening

Allergy history matters more here than for any other treatment we offer.

Please stop and speak to us before going further. A previous reaction to hyaluronidase, or a known allergy to it, means we would not normally proceed. Please call the clinic on 0800 772 0137 so we can discuss this properly — there may be alternatives, and if there are not, you need to know that before you build any expectations.
Why we ask about bee and wasp stings. Bee and wasp venom contains hyaluronidase. If you have reacted badly to a sting, you may be sensitised to it. The hyaluronidase we use is also derived from sheep, so a sheep or lanolin allergy is relevant too.
If you take a beta blocker, please tell us clearly. Beta blockers can make a severe allergic reaction harder to treat with adrenaline. It does not necessarily prevent us treating you, but we need to know beforehand and it changes how we prepare.

Risks and side effects

Dissolving carries a different and, in one respect, higher risk profile than having filler in the first place.

Common — expect these

  • Immediate swelling, often significant, particularly in the lips
  • Redness and warmth
  • Bruising, which can be extensive and last up to two weeks
  • Tenderness for several days
  • The area looking flat, deflated or older once swelling settles
  • Temporary lumpiness as the product breaks down unevenly

Less common

  • Uneven dissolving, leaving asymmetry that needs a further session
  • Over-dissolving, leaving an area flatter than you wanted
  • Incomplete dissolving, with product remaining
  • Skin looseness where tissue had been stretched
  • Infection at an injection site
  • Cold sore outbreak, where you are prone to them
  • Local allergic reaction — itching, a raised rash or swelling beyond the treated area
  • Feeling distressed or regretful about your appearance in the weeks afterwards

Rare

  • Delayed allergic reaction, appearing hours or days later
  • Persistent swelling or induration
  • Nerve irritation causing altered sensation
  • Scarring
Anaphylaxis — the risk that makes this treatment different. Hyaluronidase can cause a severe, sudden allergic reaction. It is rare, but it is the reason for the patch test, the two waiting periods, and why we ask about bee stings, sheep allergy and beta blockers.

Signs of a severe reaction: swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat beyond what was treated; difficulty breathing or swallowing; a widespread rash or hives; feeling faint, dizzy or that something is badly wrong; wheezing; or sudden severe abdominal pain.

If you are in clinic, tell us immediately — do not wait to see if it passes. If you have already left and develop any of these, call 999. Do not drive yourself. Tell them you have had an injection of hyaluronidase, then contact us.
Also contact us promptly on 0800 772 0137 if: swelling worsens rather than settles after 48 hours; you develop spreading redness, heat or discharge; you develop a temperature; or you are simply struggling with how you look and want to talk it through. That last one is not a waste of our time — please call.

Alternatives and aftercare

Alternatives

  • Waiting. Hyaluronic acid filler is not permanent. If what is bothering you is mild, letting it break down naturally over months avoids the risks entirely. This is a genuinely reasonable option and we will often suggest it.
  • Doing nothing. Sometimes what looks wrong at week two settles by week six. If you are within a few weeks of your original treatment, we may recommend waiting before deciding.
  • Correcting rather than dissolving, where asymmetry can be balanced by adding a small amount elsewhere instead.
  • Partial dissolving in a different area, where the problem is one region rather than the whole treatment — though the limits described earlier still apply.
  • A second opinion. If you are unhappy with work done elsewhere, you are entitled to seek more than one view before deciding to dissolve.

Aftercare

  • Expect swelling — it usually peaks in the first 24 to 48 hours. Cool compresses help; never put ice directly on skin.
  • No makeup over the area for 24 hours.
  • No strenuous exercise, saunas, steam rooms or hot baths for 24 hours.
  • No alcohol for 24 hours.
  • Sleep with your head slightly raised.
  • Do not massage the area unless we have told you to.
  • Antihistamines can help with itching if you get any — ask us first.
  • Avoid facials, massage, peels and laser over the area for two weeks.
  • Do not judge the result before 2 to 4 weeks. Please come back for review rather than making decisions in week one.
