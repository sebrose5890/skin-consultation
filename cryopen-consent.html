About the treatment

What CryoPen is and what happens on the day.

What it is

CryoPen is a handheld cryotherapy device that delivers a fine, controlled jet of nitrous oxide at approximately −89°C directly onto a lesion. The freeze forms ice crystals inside and between the cells, rupturing the cell membranes and destroying the targeted tissue. Your body then clears it naturally and fresh skin forms underneath.

Benign tissue is reliably destroyed from around −62°C. Liquid nitrogen works at −196°C, which is far colder than necessary and harder to control. Nitrous oxide at −89°C is more than cold enough while reducing the risk of damage to surrounding skin, and the fine applicator concentrates the freeze onto the lesion alone to within a millimetre.

We use the medical-grade CryoPen. It is a regulated medical device that non-medical practitioners are not legally permitted to operate. Several versions exist on the market and many salons use a lower-grade beauty device. Your treatment is carried out by an Independent Nurse Prescriber.

What happens

  • Each lesion takes a few seconds to a few minutes depending on size and type.
  • You will feel a sharp, cold, stinging sensation during the freeze, which settles quickly. No injections or anaesthetic are needed.
  • The lesion freezes and turns white, then thaws and flushes red.
  • Depending on the lesion, the area may blister and form a small scab over the following days.
  • The scab usually lifts within one to two weeks. Most areas are fully healed within two to six weeks.
  • Lesions on the legs typically take longer to heal.

How many sessions

Many lesions clear in a single session. Larger or more stubborn ones — particularly larger skin tags with a good blood supply, more prominent cherry angiomas and solar keratoses — often need a second or occasionally further treatments. We will advise you following clinical assessment, before any treatment is carried out.

Risks and side effects

CryoPen is very safe because of its precision, but no procedure that destroys tissue is without risk.

Common — expect these

  • A sharp stinging sensation during the freeze
  • Redness and mild swelling immediately afterwards
  • The treated area darkening in colour — this is the destroyed cells, and the darker area or scab sloughs away as healthy skin regenerates underneath
  • A blister forming, which may be clear or blood-filled
  • A small scab, lifting within one to two weeks
  • Tenderness for a day or two

Less common

  • Pigment change — a lighter or darker mark at the treated site, because freezing temporarily affects pigment-producing cells. This usually settles as the skin heals but can take months, and the risk is higher in deeper skin tones.
  • Slower healing, particularly on the legs
  • The lesion not fully clearing, needing a further session
  • Recurrence of the lesion later
  • Infection at the treated site, occasionally needing antibiotics
  • Cold sore outbreak, where you are prone to them and the treated area is near the mouth

Rare

  • Scarring, including a raised or keloid scar in those prone to them
  • Permanent loss of pigment at the treated site
  • Damage to a superficial nerve causing altered sensation, usually temporary
  • Loss of hair follicles within the treated area
  • An unexpected reaction to cold
The most serious risk is not a side effect — it is treating the wrong thing. Freezing destroys tissue. If a lesion that appeared harmless were in fact a skin cancer, freezing it would destroy the evidence needed to diagnose it and could delay treatment. This is why moles, seborrhoeic keratoses, solar keratoses and anything we are unsure about must be assessed and confirmed benign in writing before we go near them, and why we may still decline on the day.

Aftercare

Mostly this comes down to leaving it alone.

The main rule

Do not pick at the scab, or at the lesion itself. Leave it alone. Picking is the single most common cause of scarring, infection and a poor result. If a blister forms, do not burst it.

For the first few days

  • Keep the area clean and dry.
  • Avoid makeup over the treated area until the skin has closed.
  • Avoid swimming pools, saunas and steam rooms.
  • Avoid heavy exercise for 24 hours if the area is likely to rub or sweat.
  • You can return to normal activities straight away otherwise.

Until fully healed

  • Protect the area from the sun with SPF 50 daily. This is the most effective thing you can do to reduce the chance of a lasting mark.
  • No sunbeds and no self-tan over the area.
  • Avoid exfoliants, acids and retinoids over the treated skin.
  • Do not apply anything to the area unless we have advised it.

Contact us if

  • The area becomes increasingly painful, hot, swollen or produces discharge
  • Redness spreads outward from the treated site
  • You feel generally unwell with a temperature
  • The area has not healed after six weeks
  • The lesion returns
  • Anything at all concerns you
