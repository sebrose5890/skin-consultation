About the treatment

What these products are and how they differ from filler.

What it is

Juvelook and Lenisna are collagen biostimulators, not fillers. Each combines PDLLA — poly-D,L-lactic acid, a biodegradable polymer from the same family as dissolvable surgical stitches — with a small amount of non-cross-linked hyaluronic acid.

They arrive as a freeze-dried powder and are reconstituted with sterile water before injection. The hyaluronic acid gives an immediate hydration and plumping effect that fades within weeks. The PDLLA microparticles remain in the skin and stimulate your fibroblasts to produce new collagen and elastin over the following months. That collagen is the actual result — not the product itself.

The two products

  • Juvelook — 42.5mg PDLLA with smaller particles, plus 7.5mg hyaluronic acid. Injected more superficially into the dermis. Used for skin quality, fine lines, crepiness, acne scarring, and thin-skinned areas including under the eyes, crow's feet, neck and décolletage.
  • Lenisna — also sold as Juvelook Volume. 170mg PDLLA with larger particles, plus 30mg hyaluronic acid. Injected deeper for soft volume restoration and lifting across the face, around the mouth, and on the body.

Your practitioner will choose between them based on the area and what you want to achieve. Both may be used in the same treatment plan for different areas.

Timings

  • You may see a mild plumping effect immediately from the hyaluronic acid. This is temporary and will fade over the first few weeks. It is not your result, and it is normal to feel the improvement disappear before the real change begins.
  • The collagen response builds gradually from around 6 weeks and continues developing for 3 to 6 months.
  • You should not judge the outcome before the full course is complete and at least three months have passed since your last session.
Three things it is important you understand before agreeing.

1. This is a course, not a one-off. A single session will not give the result described, and each session is charged separately.

2. The immediate effect is not the result. It fades. Patients who expect filler-like instant volume are usually disappointed at week three. The genuine change arrives months later.

3. This cannot be dissolved. Hyaluronic acid filler can be removed with an enzyme. The hyaluronic acid component here can be, but the PDLLA and the collagen your body produces in response to it cannot. If you dislike the result, it must be left to resolve on its own over many months.

Regulatory status

Juvelook and Lenisna are manufactured by VAIM Inc. in South Korea. They hold Korean MFDS approval and European CE certification. They are not approved by the US FDA. You may see claims elsewhere that they are — this confuses the long-established medical use of PDLLA as a raw material with the regulatory status of these finished products. We would rather tell you accurately.

Your treatment plan

Product, areas and course.

Course and maintenance

  • Juvelook — an initial course of 3 sessions, 4 to 6 weeks apart, then maintenance around every 6 months.
  • Lenisna — an initial course of 2 to 3 sessions, 8 weeks apart, then maintenance every 18 to 24 months.

The number of sessions may vary depending on your skin, the areas treated and what you want to achieve. Your practitioner will confirm your plan with you.

A note on the under-eye area. This is one of the most rewarding areas to treat but also the least forgiving. The skin is very thin, product placed too superficially or in too great a volume can form visible lumps, and published case reports describe nodules following PDLLA in this region — particularly where a larger-particle product was used. This is why Juvelook rather than Lenisna is used here, in small volumes and conservatively. Not everyone is a suitable candidate, and where the problem is fat herniation rather than hollowing, no injectable will improve it.
We do not inject any biostimulator or filler into the frown lines between the brows. That region carries a disproportionate risk relative to any benefit. If that area concerns you, anti-wrinkle injections are the appropriate treatment and we will discuss it with you.

Risks and side effects

Every treatment carries risk. These are set out in full so you can weigh them properly.

Common — expect these

  • Redness, swelling and warmth for the first 24 to 72 hours
  • Bruising, which can last up to two weeks and is more likely around the eyes
  • Tenderness at the injection sites
  • Small raised bumps immediately after treatment, settling within hours
  • A temporary plumped appearance from the hyaluronic acid, which fades

Less common

  • Papules or nodules — small firm lumps under the skin. These can appear early or weeks to months later. Most settle on their own but some need treatment, which may include injections, oral medication or an energy-based device, and may take months to resolve.
  • Localised thickening or an uneven, patchy result needing adjustment
  • Asymmetry between sides
  • Temporary changes in skin pigmentation, lighter or darker
  • Infection at the injection site, occasionally needing antibiotics
  • Cold sore outbreak, where you are prone to them
  • A result that is weaker than hoped, or no visible benefit at all
  • Delayed inflammatory reaction, sometimes triggered by illness, dental work or vaccination months later

Rare

  • Granuloma — a persistent inflammatory lump requiring prolonged treatment
  • Abscess or deep infection
  • Blockage of a blood vessel by the product, described below
  • Skin damage or scarring following a blocked vessel
  • Allergic or hypersensitivity reaction
  • Nerve irritation causing altered sensation, usually temporary
Nodules deserve particular attention with this treatment. Any product that stimulates collagen can also produce an unwanted localised collagen response. PDLLA's rounded particle structure is designed to reduce this compared with older polylactic acid products, but the risk is not zero. It is higher in thin-skinned areas, where product is placed too superficially, and where too much is given in one session. This is why we treat conservatively and across a course rather than trying to achieve everything at once.

If you develop a lump at any point — including months later — contact us. Early treatment is far more effective than late.
Blockage of a blood vessel (vascular occlusion) Any injectable placed under the skin can enter or press on a blood vessel and interrupt the blood supply to an area. It is rare, but it needs treating within hours rather than days. Very rarely, where the vessels involved connect to those supplying the eye, this has caused visual loss.

There is no reversal agent for the PDLLA component. Unlike hyaluronic acid filler, it cannot be dissolved, so management relies on other measures and on acting fast.

Contact the clinic immediately on 0800 772 0137 if, during or after treatment, you notice: pain that is severe, worsening, or out of proportion to what you were told to expect; skin that goes white, blotchy, mottled or dusky; a lace-like or net-like purple pattern on the skin; skin that feels cold; or any change to your vision. Out of hours, or if you have visual symptoms, go to A&E and tell them you have had an injectable treatment — then call us.

Do not wait to see if it settles overnight.

Where a complication does occur, treating it may require further appointments, medication or procedures, and these may carry an additional cost.

Alternatives

You are entitled to know what else could achieve your goal, including doing nothing.

  • No treatment. Always a reasonable choice. There is no harm in declining.
  • Hyaluronic acid filler, which gives immediate volume in a single session and can be dissolved — but does not improve skin quality and does not stimulate your own collagen.
  • Other biostimulators such as Sculptra, which use a different form of polylactic acid, or calcium-based products. These work on the same principle with different characteristics.
  • Polynucleotides, which improve skin quality and repair with a different mechanism and a shorter commitment.
  • Skin boosters and injectable hydration, for hydration and glow without collagen stimulation.
  • Microneedling, peels and resurfacing, for texture, tone and superficial scarring.
  • Anti-wrinkle injections, where the concern is lines caused by movement.
  • Surgery, where the concern is significant skin laxity that no injectable will address.

Aftercare

Following this properly reduces the risk of nodules and infection.

For the first 24 hours

  • No makeup over the injection points.
  • No strenuous exercise, saunas, steam rooms or very hot baths.
  • No alcohol, which increases bruising and swelling.
  • Sleep on your back if you can, with your head slightly raised.

Massage

Follow the massage instructions your practitioner gives you on the day, and do not improvise. These products are designed not to require the intensive massage protocol used with older polylactic acid treatments, but the guidance varies by product and area. Do not massage unless you have been told to.

For two weeks

  • No facials, microneedling, peels, laser or energy-based treatments over the area.
  • Avoid dental treatment where possible, including the hygienist.
  • Avoid vaccinations where the timing is within your control.
  • Avoid sunbeds, prolonged heat and extreme cold.

Ongoing

  • Use sun protection daily. New collagen is worth protecting.
  • Stay well hydrated.
  • Attend your remaining sessions as planned — the course is what produces the result.
