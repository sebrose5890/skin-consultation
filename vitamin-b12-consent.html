About the treatment

What we use, why, and what it will and will not do.

What we use

We prescribe and administer hydroxocobalamin — the prescription-only form of vitamin B12 used by GPs and the NHS, and the form recommended in NICE guidance. It is given by intramuscular injection, usually into the upper arm.

Hydroxocobalamin binds more effectively to plasma proteins than cyanocobalamin, so it is retained in the body for longer and needs to be given less often. It is not the same as the non-prescription B12 offered by some providers. We do not use cyanocobalamin or any non-prescription form.

Why injection rather than tablets

Absorbing B12 from food or tablets depends on a protein called intrinsic factor made in the stomach. If you have pernicious anaemia, have had gastric or bowel surgery, have a digestive condition, take a stomach acid medication, or simply produce less stomach acid with age, you may absorb very little regardless of the dose you swallow. Injection bypasses that pathway entirely.

Your treatment plan

We follow NICE guidance on loading and maintenance.

  • Loading: up to 5 injections over an initial 2-week period. This builds your stores back up rather than simply topping you up.
  • Maintenance: thereafter, generally every 2 to 3 months, decided case by case based on your symptoms, your results and any underlying cause.
Will you need all five loading injections? Not everyone does, and we will decide with you as we go rather than committing you upfront.

You are more likely to need the full loading course if: your blood result showed a significant deficiency; you have symptoms affecting your nervous system such as tingling, numbness, unsteadiness or memory problems; you have a condition or surgery that prevents you absorbing B12 at all, such as pernicious anaemia or bariatric surgery; your symptoms have been present a long time; or you have had B12 injections before and found the benefit wore off quickly.

You may need fewer if: your deficiency was mild or borderline; your symptoms resolve early in the course and stay resolved; or the cause was dietary and can be corrected.

How you will know: most people who genuinely need the full course notice improvement building steadily across the injections rather than a single dramatic change. If your symptoms return within days of an injection, that generally means your stores are still low and the loading course should be completed. If you feel well after three and stay well, we may stop there. Tell us honestly how you feel between injections — that is the information we use to decide.

What it will not do

  • B12 corrects a deficiency. If you are not deficient, it is unlikely to make you feel different. It is not an energy supplement, and there is no good evidence it improves energy, mood or performance in people whose levels are normal.
  • It is not a weight loss treatment.
  • It will not treat tiredness that has another cause — thyroid problems, iron deficiency, sleep disorders, depression and many other conditions cause the same symptoms. If your B12 is normal, the answer lies elsewhere and we will say so.
  • It does not replace investigation of why you are deficient. A genuine deficiency has a cause, and that cause may need your GP's attention.

Blood testing

Strongly recommended, not mandatory — but there is a reason the timing matters.

If you are going to test, it has to be before you start. Once you begin B12 injections, your blood level will be high because of the injections. A test taken afterwards cannot tell you whether you were deficient to begin with, and it cannot be used to diagnose pernicious anaemia or any other underlying cause. That window closes as soon as the first injection is given, and it does not reopen for months.

This is why we recommend testing at your first appointment if you have not already had one. It answers the question properly — and if you turn out not to be deficient, it saves you a course of injections that were never going to help.

What we offer

We provide private blood testing in clinic, analysed by our London laboratory partner, with results typically within 24 to 48 hours. Your practitioner will advise which tests are appropriate for you — B12 alone rarely tells the whole story, and related results are usually needed to interpret it properly.

Risks and side effects

Hydroxocobalamin is very well tolerated. These are set out so your decision is properly informed.

Common — expect these

  • Soreness, redness or mild swelling at the injection site, settling within 48 hours
  • A dull ache in the arm for a day or two
  • Bruising at the injection site

Less common

  • Headache
  • Dizziness or feeling faint
  • Nausea
  • Hot flushes
  • Diarrhoea
  • Itching or a rash
  • Acne-like spots, or worsening of existing acne
  • Feeling temporarily wired or unable to sleep on the night of an injection

Rare

  • Allergic reaction, which very rarely can be severe (anaphylaxis)
  • A fall in potassium in the first days of treating a significant deficiency, which can cause palpitations, muscle weakness or cramps
  • Disturbance of heart rhythm, associated with the above
  • Infection at the injection site
  • Nerve irritation from the injection, causing temporary altered sensation
  • Worsening of gout
Contact the clinic on 0800 772 0137 if you develop: palpitations, muscle weakness or unusual cramping in the days after an injection; spreading redness, heat, swelling or discharge at the injection site; or a temperature and feeling generally unwell.

Call 999 immediately if you develop swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat, difficulty breathing, a widespread rash, or feel faint and unwell shortly after an injection. These are signs of a severe allergic reaction. Please do not drive yourself.

Alternatives and aftercare

Alternatives

  • No treatment. Always a reasonable choice, particularly if you have not been shown to be deficient.
  • Your GP. If you have a confirmed deficiency with an underlying cause such as pernicious anaemia, you are entitled to lifelong B12 on the NHS. We will always tell you if we think that is the right route for you.
  • Oral or sublingual B12, which works well where the cause is dietary and absorption is intact.
  • Dietary change, where the cause is a vegan or vegetarian diet — fortified foods and supplements can be enough.
  • Investigating other causes of your symptoms. Tiredness has many causes and B12 is only one of them.

Aftercare

  • Stay in clinic for a few minutes after your first injection so we can be sure you feel well.
  • Keep the injection site clean and dry for a few hours.
  • A little soreness is normal — gentle movement of the arm helps.
  • Paracetamol is fine if you need it.
  • Drink normally and eat as usual.
  • Tell us how you feel between injections. That is genuinely how we decide what you need next.
