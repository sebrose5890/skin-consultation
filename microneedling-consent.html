About the treatment

What medical microneedling is, and why depth is the whole point.

How it works

A medical-grade pen device creates thousands of clean, vertical micro-channels in the skin at a precisely controlled depth and speed. This triggers a controlled wound-healing response, and your fibroblasts respond by producing new collagen and elastin. It is also known as percutaneous collagen induction therapy.

Unlike laser resurfacing, it works without heat and preserves the outer layer of skin while stimulating the layer beneath. That is why recovery is shorter and why it is safe across all skin tones, including deeper tones where laser carries a higher risk of pigmentation change.

Why depth matters

  • We work at depths from 0.05mm to 2.0mm, chosen for your skin, the area and your concern.
  • Home dermarollers are legally limited to 0.2mm to 0.3mm, which reaches only the surface and cannot reach the dermis where collagen is produced.
  • Non-medical practitioners typically work at 0.5mm or less to minimise their own risk. That may improve product absorption and superficial skin quality, but it will not meaningfully treat acne scarring or laxity.
  • Genuine collagen induction for scarring and laxity requires working at 1.0mm to 2.0mm — depths that need clinical judgement and medical training to use safely.
Microneedling is not currently a regulated procedure in the UK. It can legally be performed by anyone, including those with no medical training. At Sebastian Rose all microneedling is performed by GMC or NMC-registered medical practitioners. That is not a marketing statement — it determines the depth we can safely work at, the actives we are legally able to use, and the clinical decisions we can make during your treatment.

The device

We use automated pen devices with a sterile, single-use needle cartridge for every patient. The stamping motion creates clean vertical channels, unlike a dermaroller, which drags needles across the skin at an angle and tears rather than punctures. The tip is disposed of after your treatment and never reused.

What to expect

  • Numbing cream is applied 20 to 30 minutes beforehand where we are working deeper than 0.6mm.
  • At lighter depths, most people describe a mild vibration or scratching. At deeper settings there is more sensation, particularly over thinner skin.
  • Week 1 — redness settles within 24 to 72 hours depending on depth. A glow begins to appear.
  • Weeks 2 to 4 — first visible improvements in radiance, hydration and refinement.
  • Weeks 4 to 12 — progressive collagen remodelling. Texture, firmness and tone continue improving. Scarring and pigmentation begin to soften.
  • Up to 6 months — results continue building after a completed course.
This is not a one-treatment fix, and anyone telling you otherwise is not being straight with you. A single session can give a genuine glow for an occasion. But for acne scarring, laxity, pigmentation or significant texture concerns, the evidence supports 3 to 6 sessions spaced 4 to 6 weeks apart, each charged separately. Collagen laid down in the first session matures and is built upon in the second and third. The interval is not padding — collagen remodelling is a biological process that cannot be rushed.

Risks and side effects

Medical microneedling has a strong safety profile, but it is a controlled injury and carries risk.

Common — expect these

  • Redness, like moderate sunburn. Settles in 12 to 24 hours at lighter depths, 48 to 72 hours at deeper settings.
  • Mild swelling, particularly around the eyes and after deeper treatment
  • Tightness, dryness and flaking over the following days
  • Sensitivity and warmth in the treated area
  • Pinpoint bleeding during treatment at deeper settings
  • Bruising, more likely over thin skin and bony areas

Redness is not a side effect to be endured — it is the healing response activating, and it is the mechanism through which the treatment works.

Less common

  • Small spots or a temporary breakout as the skin clears
  • Cold sore outbreak, where you are prone to them
  • Post-inflammatory pigmentation — a darker mark after healing, more likely in deeper skin tones and where sun protection is not used
  • Milia — small white bumps
  • Prolonged redness lasting more than a few days
  • Reaction to numbing cream or to an infused product
  • Infection, occasionally needing antibiotics
  • Little or no improvement, particularly from a single session
  • Flare of an underlying skin condition such as eczema or rosacea

Rare

  • Scarring, including raised or keloid scarring in those prone to it
  • Permanent pigment change
  • Granuloma or persistent inflammatory reaction to an infused product
  • Significant allergic reaction
What goes onto your skin afterwards matters as much as the treatment itself. For 24 to 48 hours the micro-channels remain open, so anything applied penetrates far deeper than normal. Many everyday skincare products — vitamin C, retinoids, acids, fragranced products — are perfectly safe on intact skin but were never designed or tested for delivery at this level. Applying the wrong thing through open channels can cause significant reactions, sensitisation and lasting damage. Please use only what we give you.

Alternatives

You are entitled to know what else could achieve your goal, including doing nothing.

  • No treatment. Always a reasonable choice. There is no harm in declining.
  • Medical skincare and daily sun protection, which underpin every other treatment and will do more over years than any single procedure.
  • Chemical peels, which resurface from the surface downward rather than stimulating from within.
  • Laser resurfacing, which can be highly effective but involves more downtime and a higher risk of pigmentation change, particularly in deeper skin tones.
  • Injectable regenerative treatments such as polynucleotides, Profhilo or skin boosters, delivered by injection rather than through micro-channels.
  • Biostimulators such as Sculptra or Juvelook, where the concern is volume and structure as well as skin quality.
  • Subcision or other procedures for tethered acne scars that microneedling alone will not lift.
Why we do not offer radiofrequency microneedling on the face. RF microneedling delivers heat into the layer beneath the skin, which causes controlled damage to subcutaneous fat. On the body that can be desirable. On the face it is not — facial fat is one of the main structural supports of a youthful appearance, and losing it is a key driver of an aged look. There is growing concern about the long-term volume consequences of repeated facial RF microneedling, particularly in younger patients. For facial collagen induction we use medical microneedling with regenerative add-ons instead. For body laxity, RF microneedling may well be appropriate and we will say so.

Aftercare

The first 48 hours are the ones that matter.

Use only the products we give you for the first 48 hours. The micro-channels are open, and anything applied goes far deeper than it would normally. No retinoids, no vitamin C, no AHAs or BHAs, no fragranced products, no makeup, no active skincare of any kind — however gentle it seems on normal skin.

For the first 24 hours

  • Do not touch your face with unwashed hands.
  • No makeup, including mineral makeup and brushes.
  • No exercise, saunas, steam rooms, swimming or very hot showers.
  • No alcohol.
  • Sleep on a clean pillowcase, on your back if you can.
  • Use only lukewarm water and the cleanser we advise.

For 48 to 72 hours

  • Continue with only the products we have provided.
  • Expect tightness, dryness and some flaking. Do not pick or exfoliate it away — let it shed naturally.
  • Reintroduce your normal skincare only when we have told you to, and reintroduce actives last.

For two weeks

  • Daily SPF 50 without exception. This is the single most important thing you can do to avoid a pigment change.
  • No sunbeds, no deliberate sun exposure, no self-tan on the area.
  • No other facial treatments, peels, waxing or laser over the area.

Contact us if

  • Redness is worsening rather than settling after 72 hours
  • The area becomes hot, increasingly painful, swollen or produces discharge
  • You develop blistering, crusting or spreading redness
  • You feel generally unwell with a temperature
  • A cold sore develops
  • Anything at all concerns you
